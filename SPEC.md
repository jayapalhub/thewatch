## 2. Competitor read and how to win

Four weekly emails already reach San Diego inboxes and none of them serves this audience. **sdtechscene.org** is the biggest calendar but cyber is one or two listings out of forty, and its Monday email is a raw list with no voice, so it cannot tell a security professional which two events matter this week. **The Social Coyote** is the best model for format (personal essay, day by day list, organizer discounts, referral reward, jobs, own flagship parties) but its audience is founders and investors, and Jonah's events are startup parties rather than peer rooms. **socalcyber.org** is a friend's early calendar covering all of Southern California with no email, and it is a cross listing partner rather than a competitor. **Luma's** Sunday email is algorithmic lifestyle picks. **Eventship** is a ticketing platform for startups.

The real competition for attention in San Diego cyber is David Spark's San Diego Cyber Group monthly dinners, ISSA San Diego's chapter events and the paid summits (FutureCon, the Official Cybersecurity Summit, Data Connectors and similar). None of those is a calendar or a newsletter. Spark and ISSA draw open, often early career and job seeking crowds, and the summits are large vendor marketing machines with six figure sponsorship budgets.

The position that wins is **the newspaper**. The Watch covers everyone's events, including Spark's dinners, ISSA, the summits and the chapter meetings, so it becomes the one email a San Diego security professional reads to know what is happening. Covering competitors generously is the strategy, not a concession: it makes the list the authority, and the authority gets to run the elevated, invitation led events above the open ones. Better Security's events then sit at the top of a calendar everyone already trusts, and the sponsor pitch becomes "a curated room of the right practitioners, drawn from the list that covers the whole city," which is the one thing the summits cannot offer and field marketers will pay for once the list proves itself.

Five things differentiate The Watch from day one. First, curation with a voice: a short note from Jerome and a "worth your evening" pick or two each week, rather than a dump of forty listings. Second, practitioner filters nobody else has: topic, CPE eligibility, cost, format and organizer. Third, a tight geographic and professional scope that keeps every listing relevant to the reader. Fourth, the organizer relationship: the submission form, add to calendar buttons and the discount code field give organizers reasons to feed The Watch first. Fifth, Better Security's own events and the "register interest" lists, which turn readers into an invitable audience.

**Send day and time: Sunday at 5:00 PM Pacific.** The reasoning is that three of the four weekly emails land Monday morning, and a Sunday evening send puts The Watch at the top of the Monday inbox stack before any of them arrive. Professionals plan their week on Sunday evening, which is exactly when a "here is what is on this week" email is useful, and registrations and RSVPs for Tuesday through Thursday events need to happen before the week fills up. Luma's Sunday email is automated and low signal, so the slot is effectively empty for curated content. The newsletter covers Monday through the following Sunday. After eight issues, run a Beehiiv A/B send comparing Sunday 5:00 PM against Tuesday 6:30 AM, which is the strongest alternative for a B2B audience, and let the open and click data decide.

## 3. Site structure

All routes are on bettersecurity.ai. Pages are static HTML rendered by a small build step or by Pages Functions reading from D1; the architecture section covers which.

| `/sandiego` | The Watch San Diego. Calendar with list and month views, filters for topic, organization, date range, cost, format and CPE. Sign up field in the header and footer. |
| `/sandiego/events/{slug}` | One page per calendar event: details, organizer, registration link, add to calendar (Google, Outlook, .ics), share link. Each listing page is what the newsletter links to, so clicks are measurable. |
| `/sandiego/organizers/{slug}` | One page per organization: description, usual cadence (for example second Tuesday), upcoming and past events, links. |

| `/events/padel-social-2027` | Event page with date, venue, agenda, pricing, sponsor logos slot, registration button (interest form now, Eventbrite later). |
| `/submit` | Organizer event submission form into the review queue. |

| `/subscribe` | Standalone sign up page for QR codes and link in bio, with the profile step on the thank you screen. |
| `/profile?t={token}` | Profile questions, reached from the thank you page and the welcome email. |
| `/about` | Jerome, Better Security, the ISACA board affiliation, how the calendar is curated. |
| `/privacy` and `/terms` | Privacy policy, consent language, mailing address, unsubscribe instructions. |

| `/go/{code}` | Redirect links used in the newsletter so clicks are counted in D1 before forwarding to the event page or organizer. |

The URL pattern `/sandiego/...` repeats for every future city.

## 4. Data model

Everything lives in one Cloudflare D1 database (SQLite). Field names are suggestions and can change in implementation; the shape is what matters.

**organizations**: id, slug, name, short_name, type (chapter, meetup, conference, vendor, university, government, community), website, contact_name, contact_email, logo_url, description, usual_cadence_text (for example "second Tuesday of the month, 6 PM"), cadence_rule (an iCal RRULE where the pattern is reliable, so the admin can suggest expected dates), default_city, default_venue_id, trusted (boolean; a trusted organizer's submissions skip to a lighter review), created_at, updated_at.

**venues**: id, slug, name, address_line, city, state, zip, neighborhood, lat, lng, website, notes.

**events**: id, slug, city (sandiego), title, organization_id, venue_id, start_at, end_at, timezone, format (in_person, virtual, hybrid), description_short (for the newsletter, under 200 characters), description_long, registration_url, cost_type (free, paid, members_free, donation), cost_min, cost_max, cost_notes, member_price_notes, cpe_eligible (boolean), cpe_hours, cpe_body (ISACA, ISC2, IIA, IAPP, other, self reported), cpe_notes, topics (JSON array from a controlled list: grc, ai, cloud, appsec, privacy, audit, identity, network, threat_intel, leadership, careers, social), audience_level (open, early_career, practitioner, senior), discount_code, discount_text, is_better_security_event (boolean), is_featured (boolean, Jerome's pick of the week), status (draft, pending_review, approved, published, cancelled, rejected, past), source (manual, submission, feed, recurrence, research), source_url, submitted_by_name, submitted_by_email, reviewer_notes, published_at, created_at, updated_at.

**submissions**: a staging copy of the events shape with the submitter's details, honeypot and rate limit metadata, and the admin decision. Approving a submission creates or updates an event; rejecting it sends a short Resend email if the submitter opted in.

**subscribers**: id, email, status (pending, active, unsubscribed, bounced), source (homepage, sandiego, padel, sponsor_page, qr, submit_form, import), consent_text_version, consented_at, ip_hash, first_name, last_name, company, role_title, seniority (student, early_career, practitioner, manager, director, executive), interests (JSON array, same list as topics), linkedin_url, tags (JSON array, for example padel_interest, dinner_interest, conference_interest, isaca_member), beehiiv_subscriber_id, profile_completed_at, profile_token, created_at, updated_at.

**event_interest**: subscriber_id, event_id or event_key (padel_2027, ai_security_conf_2027, dinner_series), created_at. This is the invite list for events that do not have registration open yet.

**sponsor_inquiries**: id, company, contact_name, email, role, interest (newsletter, event, dinner, conference, multiple), message, budget_range (optional), status (new, replied, call_booked, proposal_sent, won, lost), created_at. Also mirrored as a subscriber with the sponsor tag.

**issues**: id, issue_number, week_start, week_end, send_at, subject, preheader, personal_note (markdown), featured_event_ids, sponsor_slot (sponsor name, logo, copy, link), status (draft, pasted_to_beehiiv, sent), beehiiv_post_url, generated_html, created_at.

**link_clicks**: id, code, issue_id, event_id, target_url, clicked_at, referrer, user_agent_hash. Backs the `/go/{code}` redirects.

**topics** and **cities** can be code constants rather than tables.

## 5. How events get in

Three routes feed one review queue, and nothing publishes without Jerome's approval in the admin.

**Research backlog (Claude's job).** Before launch, build the organizations table for San Diego County by hand: ISACA San Diego, ISSA San Diego, OWASP San Diego, IIA San Diego, IAPP San Diego KnowledgeNet, ISC2 San Diego, InfraGard San Diego, Cloud Security Alliance San Diego, San Diego Cyber Group (David Spark), BSides San Diego, the Cyber Center of Excellence (CCOE), WiCyS and the university programs (SDSU, UCSD, National University, CSUSM), the recurring summits (FutureCon San Diego, Official Cybersecurity Summit, Data Connectors, Evanta, Cybersecurity Collaborative), the meetups on Meetup.com and Luma, and the vendor field events that recur locally. For each, record the usual cadence and the source page where they post dates. Then enter every known event for the next 90 days. The same research runs monthly to refresh the 90 day window, and the stored cadence rules let the admin propose expected dates for Jerome to confirm rather than hunt.

**Organizer submissions.** The `/submit` form collects the event fields in plain language, with the organizer choosing their organization from the list or adding a new one. Submissions land in the queue with a Resend notification to Jerome. Trusted organizers (a flag Jerome sets after a few good submissions) get a lighter review. The form has a honeypot field, a rate limit per IP and no file uploads in version one.

**Feeds.** Where an organizer publishes a Luma calendar, a Meetup group or an Eventbrite organizer page, a scheduled Pages Function (Cloudflare Cron Trigger, weekly) pulls new events into the queue as drafts tagged with their source, deduplicated by registration URL and by title plus date. This is a version two item because every feed needs a small adapter, and the research route covers launch.

**The review queue.** The admin shows pending items newest first with an approve, edit or reject action, a side by side with any existing event that looks like a duplicate, and one click to mark an event as the week's featured pick. Approving sets status to published and the event appears on the calendar within a minute.

## 6. The weekly workflow, under 30 minutes

The target is that Jerome's Sunday afternoon looks like this. Open `/admin/review` and clear the queue, usually five to fifteen items, at under a minute each. Open `/admin/issue/new`, which has already assembled next week's issue from published events: the day by day list for Monday through Sunday, Better Security's events block, the sponsor slot (or a house ad for the Padel Social when there is no sponsor), and the "coming up" block for events two to four weeks out that need early registration. Jerome picks one or two featured events, writes the personal note in a text box (three to six sentences, the one part that must stay human), and sets the subject line from three suggestions the page generates. The page renders the issue in Beehiiv friendly HTML with every link routed through `/go/{code}`. Jerome copies the rendered issue, pastes it into a new Beehiiv post, checks the preview on his phone, schedules it for 5:00 PM, and marks the issue as pasted in the admin. That is roughly ten minutes of review and fifteen of writing and pasting.

If the Beehiiv plan is ever upgraded to Pro, the "copy" button becomes "create draft in Beehiiv" with no other change.

The welcome email, the profile nudge, and an "events you said you were interested in" email are Beehiiv automations, built once. Padel registration opening, sponsor announcements and dinner invitations are manual sends that Jerome writes and approves.

## 7. Architecture

The stack is the compass stack with a database added, which keeps hosting free at this scale and reuses code Jerome already runs.

Cloudflare Pages hosts the site and Pages Functions handle every form post and the admin. Cloudflare D1 is the database. Cloudflare Access protects `/admin/*` with Google sign in restricted to Jerome's accounts, so there is no password code to write. A Cloudflare Cron Trigger (via a Worker or scheduled Function) runs the weekly feed pull and a nightly job that marks past events and warms the issue draft. Cloudflare Web Analytics covers page views, and the `/go/{code}` redirects plus Beehiiv's own reports cover the email side.

Public pages render in one of two ways and the choice is an implementation decision: either a Pages Function reads D1 and returns HTML with a short edge cache, which keeps a single source of truth with no build step, or a scheduled job writes static JSON that the pages fetch client side, which is closer to how compass works. The recommendation is the first, with aggressive caching, because add to calendar links, event pages and feeds all want server rendered output.

Resend sends transactional email from bettersecurity.ai (already verified): submission received, submission approved or declined, sponsor inquiry notification to Jerome, and new subscriber notification if wanted. Beehiiv sends the newsletter and automations; the subscribe Function from compass is reused with custom fields for role, seniority, company and interests, `utm_source` set per page, and double opt in turned on in Beehiiv for list quality. Unsubscribes flow back by a Beehiiv webhook to a Function that updates the subscribers table, so D1 and Beehiiv never disagree on status.

Secrets (Beehiiv key, Resend key, a webhook signing secret, an admin API token for scripts) live as encrypted Pages variables, never in the repo, exactly as compass does it. The repo is new (`jayapalhub/thewatch` or similar), plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with Functions, no framework and no build step beyond what D1 migrations need.

Brand: the Riffs shield in the 2026 electric refresh, electric blue #1FB6F0, shield blue #1B4A8A, deep navy #071426 for dark sections, white and a light grey for the calendar surface. The tone is a fun tech startup rather than a consultancy, with the tagline reworked for events (working line: "Know what's happening in San Diego security").
